Definition

What is an open-weight model License Explorer?

The OpenWeightModels License Explorer is a model-by-model comparison of the legal terms attached to downloadable AI weights. It does not infer rights from the words “open”, “community”, “research” or “open weight”.

For each checkpoint, the explorer records the exact license or terms document, commercial-use status, modification rights, redistribution conditions and material special clauses. This matters because two models with equally downloadable weights can create very different obligations for a company building an API, redistributing a derivative, fine-tuning a model or serving millions of users.

The explorer also distinguishes the model license from a separate acceptable-use or safety policy. Where a license incorporates such a policy by reference — as Meta's Llama Community agreements do — that relationship is surfaced. Where a publisher releases model weights under Apache 2.0 and separately publishes a usage policy, the site does not rewrite Apache 2.0 to pretend the two documents are the same instrument.

What we compare

Seven questions that matter in production.

A one-word license label is insufficient for model governance. These dimensions are stored independently in each Passport.

01

Commercial use

Can an organization use the weights in a revenue-generating product or service, and does a scale threshold change that right?

02

Modification

Can the model be fine-tuned, merged, adapted or otherwise modified under the published terms?

03

Redistribution

What must accompany redistributed weights or derivatives — license text, notices, attribution or naming?

04

Hosted service

Does exposing functionality over an API/web service count as distribution under the exact terms?

05

Derivative models

Are there naming or attribution conditions when outputs or materials are used to train another AI model?

06

Usage policies

Is an acceptable-use document separate, optional, publisher guidance, or explicitly incorporated into the agreement?

07

Scale & geography

Do user-count, revenue or regional conditions alter eligibility or trigger additional obligations?

Rule

Exact checkpoint

Licenses are attached to a model release, not assumed from a neighboring family member. Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen2.5-Coder are a useful example.

Open source vs open weight

A license is only part of the openness question.

Apache 2.0 and MIT are OSI-approved software licenses, but calling an entire AI system “Open Source AI” involves more than seeing one of those labels on a weight repository.

The Open Source Initiative's Open Source AI Definition 1.0 describes freedoms to use, study, modify and share the system and also addresses access to the preferred form for modification. OpenWeightModels therefore avoids turning a software-license field into a universal “open-source AI” badge.

Conversely, a custom model license does not make the weights “closed” if the trained parameters are publicly obtainable. The registry uses open weight as the access category, then separately documents the exact rights.

Special cases

Why the exact terms change deployment decisions.

These examples show why the explorer stores clauses, not only SPDX-like names.

Llama Community Licenses

Meta's Llama 3.3 and Llama 4 agreements require “Built with Llama” attribution for specified distribution scenarios, include Notice-file requirements and contain a 700M-MAU additional commercial condition. Llama 4 also incorporates an AUP with a multimodal EU developer restriction.

Gemma Terms

Google's Gemma Terms define “Distribution” broadly enough to include making Gemma or derivatives available as a Hosted Service. That makes hosted deployment a legal dimension worth surfacing, not only an infrastructure choice.

Qwen2.5-VL

The Qwen License includes a separate authorization condition above 100M monthly active users for specified commercial use and requires “Built with Qwen” or “Improved using Qwen” in certain derived-model scenarios.

Kimi K2

Moonshot's Modified MIT license preserves broad MIT-style permissions but adds prominent “Kimi K2” UI branding above either 100M MAU or $20M monthly revenue.

Apache 2.0

Apache 2.0 grants broad rights and includes an express patent license, while redistribution retains license/NOTICE obligations where applicable. A separate publisher usage policy should be recorded separately unless legally incorporated.

MIT

The MIT License grants broad use, modification, distribution, sublicensing and sale rights subject to preserving the copyright and permission notice in copies or substantial portions.

FAQ

License questions, separated.

Can all open-weight models be used commercially?

No universal rule exists. In this 32-model registry many checkpoints use Apache 2.0 or MIT, while Llama, Gemma, Qwen2.5-VL and Kimi K2 use terms with additional conditions. Read the exact model release.

Is Apache 2.0 more “open” than MIT?

Both are OSI-approved permissive licenses. They differ in text and obligations, including Apache 2.0's express patent provisions. OpenWeightModels does not rank one as an overall openness winner.

Does a custom license mean the weights are closed?

Not in this registry's terminology. If trained weights are publicly obtainable they can qualify as open-weight here, while the custom terms are documented separately.

Is this legal advice?

No. The explorer is a technical comparison and provenance layer. Current official legal text and organization-specific legal review remain authoritative for a particular use.
